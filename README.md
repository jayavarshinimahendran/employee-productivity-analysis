Employee Productivity Analysis

1. Case Study Title

Employee Productivity Analysis Using Google Sheets

2. Problem Statement

The main aim of this case study is to analyse employee productivity based on their attendance percentage and the number of tasks completed. It helps to identify productive departments, understand employee performance and find employees who need improvement in attendance.

3. Objectives

- To analyse employee attendance and productivity.
- To calculate the total and average tasks completed.
- To classify employees based on their attendance percentage.
- To compare productivity across different departments.
- To identify employees with low attendance.
- To represent the analysis using charts and visualizations.

4. Dataset Description

The dataset contains information about 12 employees from three departments: Development, Testing and HR.

The dataset includes the following columns:

- Employee ID
- Employee Name
- Department
- Attendance Percentage
- Tasks Completed
- Attendance Class

Attendance is classified into three categories:

- Excellent – 90% and above.
- Satisfactory – 75% to 89%.
- Poor – Below 75%.

5. Spreadsheet Functions Used

The following spreadsheet functions were used:

- IF() – To classify employees based on attendance percentage.
- AVERAGE() – To calculate average attendance.
- SUM() – To calculate total tasks completed.
- MAX() – To find the maximum number of tasks.
- MIN() – To find the minimum number of tasks.
- COUNT() – To count the total number of employees.
- COUNTIF() – To count employees based on specific conditions.
- COUNTIFS() – To count employees satisfying multiple conditions.
- AVERAGEIF() – To calculate department-wise average values.
- SUMIF() – To calculate department-wise total tasks.
- RANK() – To rank employees based on tasks completed.
- CORREL() – To identify the relationship between attendance and productivity.
- INDEX() and MATCH() – To identify the department and employee with the highest productivity.

6. Data Analysis Techniques Performed

The following analysis techniques were performed:

- Employee attendance classification.
- Sorting employees based on tasks completed.
- Filtering employees with attendance below 75%.
- Grouping employees department-wise.
- Department-wise productivity analysis.
- Calculation of average attendance and total tasks.
- Identification of maximum and minimum task completion.
- Correlation analysis between attendance and productivity.
- Conditional formatting to highlight low attendance.
- Heat map analysis for department-wise productivity.

7. Charts and Visualizations Created

The following charts and visualizations were created:

1. Department-wise Average Productivity: Compares average tasks completed by employees in each department.
2. Employee Distribution by Department: Shows the distribution of employees across Development, Testing and HR.
3. Histogram – Distribution of Employee Attendance: Represents employees across different attendance ranges.
4. Scatter Chart – Attendance vs Tasks Completed: Shows the relationship between employee attendance and productivity.
5. Heat Map: Highlights department-wise productivity using colour scales.

 8. Important Observations

- The dataset contains 12 employees from three departments.
- The overall average attendance is 85.08%.
- The total number of tasks completed is 411.
- Five employees have Excellent attendance.
- Five employees have Satisfactory attendance.
- Two employees have Poor attendance.
- Development department has the highest average productivity with 38.2 tasks per employee.
- Testing department has an average productivity of 35.75 tasks per employee.
- HR department has an average productivity of 25.67 tasks per employee.
- Indhu from Development completed the highest number of tasks (48) with 98% attendance.
- Charan and Gokul have attendance below 75%.
- The correlation between attendance and tasks completed is approximately 0.82, showing a strong positive relationship.

9. Conclusion

This case study helped analyse employee productivity using spreadsheet functions, data analysis techniques and visualizations. The analysis shows that the Development department has the highest average productivity. It also identifies employees with low attendance and highlights the relationship between attendance and task completion.

Overall, this project demonstrates how Google Sheets can be used to organize data, perform calculations, analyse employee performance and make better decisions for improving productivity.
